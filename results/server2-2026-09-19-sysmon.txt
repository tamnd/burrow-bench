The answer is that the monitor costs nothing this suite can see. Every
column moves both ways across rounds and no tree is consistently on either
side of another. Round 2 of the last table has sysmon ahead on three of the
four rows, which is not a speedup and is the clearest statement in the file
of how wide the noise is.

Two readings above came back with a spread over 100 percent and are left in
rather than dropped: sysmon yield_pair in round 1 of the last table, and
sysmon yield_pair in round 3 of the middle one. Both were interrupted runs
and neither is a measurement. Dropping them would have made the file look
tidier and would have been the wrong file.

The part worth keeping is how this went wrong first. The original comparison
was this branch measured today against results/server2-2026-09-19-sched.txt,
which was taken on the same machine on the same day while it was idle, and
it showed goroutine_yield_alone 45 percent worse with the monitor in,
repeatably, twice. It was the machine. The load average went from under one
to over seven between the two measurements, and every row moved with it. The
regression disappeared the moment the variants were built side by side and
interleaved, and nothing about the code changed in between.

So: a results file is a measurement of a machine at a moment, and comparing
two of them taken hours apart on a shared box measures the hours. Build both
trees, interleave the runs, and read the rounds against each other.
